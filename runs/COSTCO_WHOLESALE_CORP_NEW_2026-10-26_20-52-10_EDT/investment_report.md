## Overview

Costco Wholesale Corporation (COST) operates a membership-based wholesale warehouse club model. The company generated revenue from two sources: merchandise net sales and membership fees. For fiscal year 2026 (52 weeks ended August 30, 2026), net sales were "$297.2 billion" and membership fees were "$5,907" million, for total revenue of "$303,154" million (Earnings Release L75, L81, L86).

The physical footprint spans 11 countries: "Costco currently operates 939 warehouses, including 647 in the United States and Puerto Rico, 115 in Canada, 43 in Mexico, 37 in Japan, 29 in the United Kingdom, 20 in Korea, 15 in Australia, 14 in Taiwan, seven in China, five in Spain, three in France, two in Sweden, and one each in Iceland, and New Zealand" (Earnings Release L45). The company also "operates e-commerce sites in the U.S., Canada, the U.K., Mexico, Korea, Taiwan, Japan, Australia, and China" (Earnings Release L45).

Operating segments are three: the United States, Canada, and Other International, as reflected in the XBRL segment tags (10-K L270–L279, L314–L324).

## Investment Recommendation

Costco delivered a clean FY2026: revenue grew 10.14% to $303,154 M, diluted EPS rose 14.0% to $20.76, and return on average equity reached 28.40% on a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.172—the lowest in its peer set. The digitally-enabled comparable-sales rate of roughly 20.7% for the full year signals a structural growth vector that is compounding faster than the 6.6% total-company comp, and capex acceleration ($6,435 M, +17%) confirms management is funding the next wave of warehouses across 939 locations in 11 countries.

The offsetting concern is valuation. At the user-supplied price of $1,005.16, the stock trades at **48.42×** trailing diluted EPS, a rich multiple that prices in sustained double-digit earnings growth for several years. Furthermore, Q4 EPS of $6.75 was flattered by a non-recurring $0.15-per-share IEEPA tariff-refund benefit, so the recurring run-rate is marginally below the headline. Operating margin of 3.85% remains in line with WMT (4.18%) and BJ (3.81%), leaving little room for margin expansion to de-risk the multiple.

The quality of the franchise—best-in-class ROE, lowest leverage, superior growth, and a digital tailwind—argues against a Sell. But a 48.42× trailing multiple on a 3.85% operating-margin business, with a one-time item embedded in the latest quarter, does not offer a compelling risk-reward for a new position at the current price. Existing holders have a durable compounder; new capital is better deployed elsewhere until the multiple compresses or growth re-accelerates.

**Rating: Hold**
