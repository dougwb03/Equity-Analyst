## Overview

(NYSE: EAF) is a graphite-electrode and by-products manufacturer incorporated in Switzerland ("GrafTech Switzerland S.A.," 10-K.txt:L7182) and headquartered in Brooklyn Heights, Ohio (8-K Exhibit 99.1:L11). The company operates in two reported revenue segments: **Graphite Electrodes** and **By-Products and Other**, as reflected in the 10-K XBRL taxonomy tags `gti:GraphiteElectrodesMember` and `gti:ByProductsAndOtherMember` (10-K.txt:L229–L251). ## Future Developments and Strategic Recommendations

### a) Monterrey, Mexico Facility Closure

The company "announced planned closure of Monterrey, Mexico manufacturing facility, expected to generate $20 to $25 million in annual cash cost savings while better aligning production capacity with market demand" (8-K Exhibit 99.1:L33). Q3 2026 results included "$14 million of restructuring and other exit charges, the majority of which are non-cash, associated with the planned closure of our Monterrey facility" (8-K Exhibit 99.1:L142). The CEO characterized the move as "a significant step toward improving GrafTech's long-term competitiveness and financial performance" that will "lower costs and improve operating efficiency" (8-K Exhibit 99.1:L36).

### b) Realized-Pricing Recovery

The earnings release headline states the company is "Expecting higher realized pricing in the fourth quarter and in 2027" (8-K Exhibit 99.1:L9). Management "reaffirm[ed] full-year 2026 volume and cost expectations" (8-K Exhibit 99.1:L10), implying the Q3 pricing softness is viewed as transitory.

### c) Cost-Structure Simplification

Cash COGS per MT has declined sequentially and year-over-year (Q3 2025: $3,795 → Q3 2026: $3,653; 8-K Exhibit 99.1:L1082, L1078). The Monterrey closure is expected to accelerate this trend by removing a higher-cost plant from the portfolio.

### d) Debt-Structure Reset

The December 2024 refinancing introduced new first-lien term loan and second-lien notes (10-K.txt:L70–L80, L7206–L7208), with associated "debt modification costs" still being amortized into interest expense in 2026 (8-K Exhibit 99.1:L1098, L1104). Total liquidity stood at $242 million as of 9/30/2026, including $134 million of cash (8-K Exhibit 99.1:L31).

### Key Risks Evident in the Filings

1. **Pricing cyclicality.** Revenue is directly tied to weighted-average realized pricing, which declined in Q3 2026 and is the primary driver of the EBITDA compression (8-K Exhibit 99.1:L141, L145).
2. **Leverage and interest burden.** Four note tranches plus a first-lien term loan create substantial fixed-charge obligations (10-K.txt:L70–L80, L7206). The stockholders' deficit (10-K.txt:L7239) underscores cumulative loss accumulation.
3. **Cash-burn trajectory.** Nine-month 2026 operating cash flow was negative $92.0 million and adjusted FCF was negative $118.7 million (8-K Exhibit 99.1:L88, L125). Even with $242 million of total liquidity (8-K Exhibit 99.1:L31), sustained negative FCF pressures the balance sheet.
4. **Execution risk on restructuring.** The $20–25 million annual savings from Monterrey (8-K Exhibit 99.1:L33) are forward-looking and subject to implementation; $14 million of charges have already been recognized (8-K Exhibit 99.1:L142).
5. **By-product exposure.** The "By-Products and Other" segment (10-K.txt:L241–L251) and its COGS line (8-K Exhibit 99.1:L1057–L1063, $30.4 million in 9M 2026) add revenue and cost volatility not directly tied to electrode pricing.

## Investment Recommendation

**Rating: Sell**

GrafTech enters 2027 as a structurally impaired graphite-electrode manufacturer. FY 2025 produced a net loss of ($219,835,000) and pushed stockholders' equity to a deficit, while the company carries $1,094,706,000 in long-term debt—more than double its annual revenue. The Q3 2026 print confirms the pricing trough: nine-month adjusted EBITDA swung to negative $3.0 million and adjusted free cash flow deepened to negative $118.7 million against a total liquidity cushion of only $242 million. No peer in the supplied set (HXL, MTRN, CRS) shares GrafTech's negative equity, negative operating cash flow, or negative operating margin (−15.29 %).

Management points to a realized-pricing inflection and the Monterrey closure ($20–$25 million annual savings) as the path to 2027 recovery. Those are credible catalysts, but they are forward-looking and execution-dependent, while the balance-sheet damage is already locked in. At the current cash-burn pace, the $242 million liquidity buffer provides limited runway to bridge any further pricing softness before a refinancing event becomes necessary.

**Deciding number:** The financial analyst reports stockholders' equity at **($259,628,000)** (10-K, `us-gaap:StockholdersEquity`, as of 2025-12-31). A negative equity base of this magnitude, layered beneath roughly $1.1 billion of senior and second-lien debt, means the equity holder is absorbing all downside in a company that is, on a book basis, insolvent. Until a confirmed pricing recovery and the Monterrey savings are evidenced in reported EBITDA, the risk/reward skew favors exiting.

**Sell.**
