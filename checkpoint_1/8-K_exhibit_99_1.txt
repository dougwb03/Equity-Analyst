Sales volume for the third quarter of 2026 was 28.8 thousand MT, flat compared to the third quarter of 2025. For the first nine months of 2026, sales volume increased 7% year-over-year, consistent with our full-year growth expectations. For the third quarter of 2026, our weighted-average realized price was approximately $3,900 per MT, a 7% decrease compared to the third quarter of 2025 and flat compared to the second quarter of 2026.
Production volume was 27.0 thousand MT for the third quarter of 2026, resulting in a capacity utilization rate of 64%.  While production volume has exceeded sales volume for the first nine months of 2026, we continue to expect production and sales volume levels to be balanced on a full-year basis.
In September 2026, we announced the planned closure of our graphite electrode manufacturing facility in Monterrey, Mexico, as part of our ongoing efforts to optimize our manufacturing footprint and improve long-term profitability. Following the phased wind-down of operations, customer requirements currently served by Monterrey will transition to our facilities in Calais, France and Pamplona, Spain, with connecting pin production capacity transferred to Pamplona. Upon completion, our annual graphite electrode production capacity is expected to be approximately 127 thousand MT. We expect this initiative to generate $20 to $25 million in annual cash cost savings, excluding one-time costs, with the full annual benefit expected to be realized in 2028. In addition to strengthening our cost structure, this action will improve operating efficiency and better align our production capacity with market demand.

Outlook
Global steel demand, outside of China, is projected to grow modestly in 2026, with anticipated growth in many of our key commercial regions. In the United States, modest demand growth, coupled with favorable trade policies, has driven a 6% increase in steel production year-to-date through August. In Europe, while steel production is flat over this period, the steel market outlook is improving, reflecting recently approved increases in trade protections.
Based on current steel market conditions, we continue to expect modest growth in graphite electrode demand for the full year of 2026. For GrafTech, we are maintaining our expectation for a 5–10% year-over-year increase in graphite electrode sales volume as we continue to gain market share.
We are encouraged by improving pricing trends across our key commercial regions and believe we have reached an inflection point in our realized pricing. During the third quarter and early in the fourth quarter, we have secured customer commitments at higher price levels than comparable commitments secured in earlier periods. We expect these higher-priced commitments to contribute to improved realized graphite electrode pricing as they begin to flow through our results. For the fourth quarter, we anticipate a mid-single-digit percentage increase in our weighted-average realized pricing compared to the third quarter. We believe this momentum, together with our disciplined commercial approach and ongoing efforts to optimize our product and customer mix, positions us for meaningfully higher realized pricing in 2027. We remain focused on supporting sustainable pricing improvement through our previously announced price increases, trade enforcement efforts in key jurisdictions and strategic actions to better align industry supply with market demand, including our recently announced plan to close our Monterrey, Mexico manufacturing facility.
Despite continued cost pressures from oil-based raw materials, energy and logistics, our ongoing cost reduction and operational efficiency initiatives are expected to offset these headwinds. Accordingly, we continue to expect a low-single-digit percentage decline in our cash cost of goods sold per MT for 2026 compared to 2025.
We remain focused on disciplined capital spending and working capital management. For 2026, we continue to expect a modest increase in working capital to support higher sales volume. We also continue to anticipate full-year capital expenditures of approximately $35 million.
Longer term, we remain confident in the structural drivers of demand growth for graphite electrodes, including the ongoing transition toward electric arc furnace steelmaking, as well as opportunities for petroleum needle coke in battery applications. We believe our strategic initiatives to improve our cost structure, optimize our manufacturing footprint and strengthen our commercial position, combined with our vertical integration and differentiated capabilities, position GrafTech to deliver stronger financial performance as market conditions improve.

About GrafTech
GrafTech International Ltd. is a leading global manufacturer of high-quality graphite electrode products essential to the production of electric arc furnace steel and other ferrous and non-ferrous metals. With 140 years of experience in carbon science and manufacturing, GrafTech operates a competitive portfolio of large-scale, low-cost, ultra-high power graphite electrode facilities and is the only major graphite electrode producer vertically integrated into petroleum needle coke, its key raw material. This unique position provides important advantages in product quality, supply reliability, and cost competitiveness. Building on its core graphite electrode and needle coke capabilities, GrafTech also has deep experience in research and development, engineering and processing of specialized carbon and graphite materials supporting selected applications in industrial manufacturing, energy storage, anode materials, defense, and other emerging technologies.

Non‑GAAP Financial Measures
In addition to providing results that are determined in accordance with GAAP, we have provided certain financial measures that are not in accordance with GAAP. EBITDA, adjusted EBITDA, adjusted net loss, adjusted loss per share, free cash flow, adjusted free cash flow, net debt and cash cost of goods sold per MT are non-GAAP financial measures.
We define EBITDA, a non‑GAAP financial measure, as net loss plus interest expense, minus interest income, plus income taxes and depreciation and amortization. We define adjusted EBITDA, a non-GAAP financial measure, as EBITDA adjusted by any pension and other post-employment benefit ("OPEB") expenses, non‑cash gains or losses from foreign currency remeasurement of non‑operating assets and liabilities in our foreign subsidiaries where the functional currency is the U.S. dollar, stock-based compensation expense, gains on asset sales, restructuring and other exit charges and Tax Receivable Agreement adjustments. Adjusted EBITDA is the primary metric used by our management and the Company's Board of Directors (the "Board of Directors") to establish budgets and operational goals for managing our business and evaluating our performance.
We monitor adjusted EBITDA as a supplement to our GAAP measures, and believe it is useful to present to investors, because we believe that it facilitates evaluation of our period‑to‑period operating performance by eliminating items that are not operational in nature, allowing comparison of our recurring core business operating results over multiple periods unaffected by differences in capital structure, capital investment cycles and fixed asset base. In addition, we believe adjusted EBITDA and similar measures are widely used by investors, securities analysts, ratings agencies, and other parties in evaluating companies in our industry as a measure of financial performance and debt‑service capabilities.
We define adjusted net loss, a non‑GAAP financial measure, as net loss, excluding the items used to calculate adjusted EBITDA and further excluding debt modification costs, less the tax effect of those adjustments and non-cash income tax expense related to the establishment of a deferred tax valuation allowance. We define adjusted loss per share, a non‑GAAP financial measure, as adjusted net loss divided by the weighted average diluted common shares outstanding during the period. We believe adjusted net loss and adjusted loss per share are useful to present to investors because we believe that they assist investors’ understanding of the underlying operational profitability of the Company.
We define free cash flow, a non-GAAP financial measure, as net cash provided by or used in operating activities less capital expenditures. We define adjusted free cash flow, a non-GAAP financial measure, as free cash flow adjusted by payments made for restructuring and debt modification costs. We use free cash flow and adjusted free cash flow as critical measures in the evaluation of liquidity in conjunction with related GAAP amounts. We also use these measures when considering available cash, including for decision-making purposes related to dividends and discretionary investments. Further, these measures help management, the Board of Directors, and investors evaluate the Company's ability to generate liquidity from operating activities.
